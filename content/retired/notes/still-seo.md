# Google says it is still SEO. Google is right about Google.

On 15 May 2026, Google published a guide to appearing in its AI features and put one sentence at the centre of it: optimising for generative AI search is "still SEO". The same guide says you do not need machine-readable AI files or Markdown, do not need to chunk content, do not need to write in a special way for AI, do not need any special schema.org markup, and that seeking inauthentic mentions "isn't as helpful as it might seem".

A prospect can now cite Google against anyone selling a standalone "GEO" service. Good. Here is what we say when they do.

## We agree with all five.

We agreed with every one of the five before the guide was published.

The llms.txt point is the clearest. In a server-log study across 137,210 domains, 97% of valid llms.txt files received zero requests in a month. A file nobody fetches is not a signal. It has one legitimate life, as a documentation index for coding agents, and we use it there and nowhere else.

Chunking and AI-specific schema fail the same test from the other direction. Across 45 studies of generative engine optimisation reviewed in July 2026, the only tactics with replicated support were topical relevance and position, extractable statistics, quotes and definitions, and recency. Formatting alone was not among them. The widely repeated "+40% visibility" figure comes from an experiment in which the source was already in the model's context, which is not a condition anyone's website enjoys.

Seeded mentions are the one Google warns against by name. Selling that is selling a liability with an invoice attached.

## The two surfaces do not share their sources.

The guide governs Google's surfaces. On those, the craft that earns a ranking is the craft that earns a citation. Crawlable pages. A clear fact a machine can lift. Real authority.

But the surfaces are not the same, and the guide does not claim they are.

On an 11,500-query benchmark, the sources Google organic and Google's AI answers draw on overlap at below 0.2, measured at the URL level. AI answers appear on 51.5% of representative real-user queries. Sites that block Google's AI crawler are significantly less likely to be retrieved by AI answers even when their content is otherwise accessible. And ChatGPT and Perplexity, which the guide does not govern at all, are measured separately, because engines disagree.

"Still SEO" is a statement about craft. It is true. It is not a statement that the surfaces retrieve the same pages, because they do not, and it is not a statement about who wins on each, because that is different too.

## What that means for the work.

One craft, measured per surface.

The craft is what the guide describes. The measurement is where most of the category falls down, because a single AI-visibility run has a confidence interval of roughly −22% to +122%, and the standard error only drops below 0.10 at seven runs per prompt. Anyone reporting one run is reporting a coin flip and calling it a ranking.

So we run seven. We report a band per engine, never blended, with the prompt count, runs, window, tool and cost printed under it. And we agree with Google, in public, every time someone asks.
