# Eight things the GEO category sells that do not survive scrutiny.

The GEO agency category is roughly a year old and already has a set of stock deliverables and stock statistics. We checked them against their sources. Four of the deliverables are things Google has explicitly said you do not need. Four of the statistics trace to nothing, or to a source that says something else.

We do not sell any of the eight. This is the list, with the reasons, so that you can ask any agency about them.

## Four deliverables.

**1. llms.txt as a search signal.**
97% of valid llms.txt files received zero requests in May 2026, across 137,210 domains. Google's guide says you do not need it to appear in Search. The file has one real use, as a documentation index for coding agents, and any agency selling it as a ranking lever is selling a file nobody fetches.

**2. "Chunking" content for AI.**
Google: there is no requirement to break content into pieces for AI. The critical survey of 45 GEO studies found no replicated support for formatting alone as a lever; the levers with support are topical relevance and position, extractable statistics, quotes and definitions, and recency.

**3. AI-specific schema markup.**
Google: there is no special schema.org markup to add for AI features. Structured data still earns rich results, and that is what it is for. Sold as an AI lever, it is a line item for something that does not exist.

**4. Bought or seeded brand mentions.**
Google warns against inauthentic mentions in the same guide. A deliverable Google warns against by name is a liability, not a deliverable.

## Four numbers.

**5. "GEO delivers +40% visibility."**
The figure comes from the foundational GEO paper, and it is conditional on the source already being in the model's context window. It establishes neither organic discoverability nor a traffic effect. Quoted without the condition, it is a different claim from the one the paper made.

**6. Any "$X to $Y a month for GEO" price band.**
Every one we traced leads to an agency rate card citing another agency rate card. There is no survey behind any of them. When a price band has no sample, it is not a benchmark; it is an anchor.

**7. A share-of-voice percentage from a tool that will not disclose its sampling.**
Most tools sample an API rather than the interface your customers use, and Google states in its own documentation that "no third-party tool has access to our internal ranking or AI systems." A number without its method is decoration.

**8. A single-run "AI ranking".**
The same prompt, same engine, same day, returns a different set of sources each time. A single run's detection rate has a 95% confidence interval of roughly −22% to +122%. The standard error only drops below 0.10 at seven runs per prompt. One run is a coin flip with a dashboard on it.

## What survives.

The unglamorous things. Crawlable pages, with the named AI crawlers admitted by name. Pages a machine can lift a fact from: a statistic, a quotation, a price, a date. Topical relevance and a real position on the topic. Recency. Brand search strength. And a measurement that runs seven times and reports a band.

None of it is new. That is the point Google made, and we agree with it.
